CONTENT:
Refraction

He woke into the same thought twice.
It looked familiar enough to trust.

I watched him notice it—
the flicker behind the eyes,
the way confusion arrived quietly,
as if it had been there all along.

The room was polite at first.
The air waited for its cue.
The clock rehearsed a minute
and performed it again,
almost flawlessly.

He moved as though following instructions
written somewhere out of sight.
Every gesture returned to its beginning.

When he looked toward the mirror,
I leaned in.
The glass shimmered like language
just before meaning.

He raised a hand.
So did I.
Between us, the delay stretched thin
and hummed.

I blinked.
He didn’t.

Light pooled in the corners—
a tide of sleep or memory,
I couldn’t tell which.

He tried to write something down.
The pen trembled like a live wire,
then stilled—
its ink forming circles,
patient and endless.

I wrote too, to steady myself,
but the words repeated
before I finished them.
The sound of my breathing
came from his mouth.

He whispered something.
I heard it behind my teeth.

The ceiling sighed.
The floor answered.
The walls forgot their angles
and leaned closer for warmth.

I tried to step back
but the distance folded like paper.
He was closer now,
breathing with my lungs.

I said his name.
He mouthed mine.

The light blinked,
and both of us dreamed the other first.

I woke into the same thought twice.
It looked familiar enough to trust.
