He Waits Between

He waited where the mist thinned,
hands resting on the oar
as though it wanted to escape.

The river made no sound
that could be called warning.
It simply opened.

I told him I wasn’t ready.
He nodded, the way old trees do in wind—
not agreement,
just acknowledgment.

The lantern’s flame wavered,
casting a light too patient to comfort.

When I asked if there was pain,
he smiled,
and the mist brightened around his face.
Not warmth—
something gentler, like permission.

“You’ve carried the hard part,” he said.
“The rest knows where to go.”

The oar touched water.
It sounded like forgetting.

The cold settled beside me,
uninvited but kind,
a familiar hand on my shoulder.

I didn’t notice when we left the shore,
only that the stars were moving closer,
and I no longer needed to breathe
to count them.

He looked toward the far bank,
where the fog turned silver and thin.
“You’ll like the quiet,” he said,
“it asks nothing of you.”
