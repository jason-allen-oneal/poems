CONTENT:
The Borrowed Room

The morning light entered on cue,
as if the windows had learned the script.
The coffee steamed in a cup
I didn’t remember buying.

The photographs on the wall
watched with polite indifference.
They knew the story better than I did.

Every object performed familiarity—
keys, shoes, the hum of the refrigerator.
Even my reflection
looked comfortable pretending.

I lived carefully,
not to disturb the rhythm
that had been arranged for me.

But the air began to listen.
Its stillness thickened,
heavy with rehearsal.

The house sighed through its seams,
whispering in frequencies
too low for memory to catch.

Each night the clock reset itself to dusk.
The shadows returned to their positions,
slightly improved.

I found a photograph of myself
standing where I stood,
only the walls behind me
were breathing.

Once, I heard footsteps
in perfect counterpoint to mine.
When I stopped, they didn’t.

I tried locking every door,
but the latches clicked from the other side,
as if to reassure me
they’d already done it.

Now the air tastes of dust and marrow.
The wallpaper peels back in thin, deliberate curls,
listening for confession.

I wait for the house to speak
the name it keeps almost saying.
I practice stillness.
I let the lights flicker their approval.

At night, the house exhales
in someone else’s sleep.
I listen,
and answer when called.
