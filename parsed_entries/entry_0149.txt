Part 1: The Law of Diminishing Flame

I built a tower from intentions,
each stone the same apology,
worn smooth by repetition.

The elements never argued—
they simply erased symmetry.

I sharpened purpose into method,
believed friction might ignite meaning.
It didn’t.
Fire eats, but it doesn’t remember.

The sun forgets to rise for reasons
we call logic.
Vines still climb failed monuments.
The lesson isn’t mercy—
it’s that the universe takes without remorse.

So I set the last stone,
feel its weight keenly,
and let the wind finish what I started.
