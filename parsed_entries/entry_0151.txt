Part 3: Apocrypha of the Remaining

They say a man once built a tower
and named it reality.
The stones forgot his name
but kept his fingerprints.

The wind carried whispers
of one who stopped pleading with the ether
and called that silence peace.

Now the dust arranges itself
in patterns too deliberate for chance.
Some call it memory,
others, gravity pretending to care.

I’ve seen his reflection
flicker in still puddles—
not a ghost, only a habit
the universe has yet to break.

I inherit his resolve.
It fits like old armor:
heavy, imperfect,
but proven against the world.
